# Game Design Document

## Project

Breaking Chains: Blood and Cotton is a mature, historically grounded open-world action-adventure set in the antebellum United States. The game begins as a personal revenge story and expands into a regional resistance campaign.

## Fixed canon

- Country: United States.
- Period: 1830–1834.
- Opening context: aftermath of Nat Turner's Rebellion in Southampton County, Virginia, August 1831.
- Opening location: fictional Ashgrove Plantation.
- Protagonist: Ezra Coffin, fictional enslaved blacksmith, age 28.
- Technical acts: Escape, The Hunt, Reckoning, Breaking the System.
- World: five connected regions, approximately 45 square miles.
- Canonical outcome: regional victory and 340 people liberated; slavery is not ended nationally.
- Voice language: English.
- Repository language: English.

## Narrative structure

The visible narrative structure differs from the technical act structure:

1. Prologue: Ashgrove.
2. The Names: Ezra's revenge list and the first target investigations.
3. The Roads: pursuit, refuges, routes, rescues, and community trust.
4. The Ledger: legal, commercial, and documentary investigation.
5. The Network: coordinated regional resistance.
6. What Remains: the regional victory and its costs.

This structure begins with the energy of a revenge thriller but turns the list into a map of the system that made the crimes possible.

## Target-list premise

Ezra initially records five names connected to the destruction of his family:

- Ashgrove overseer: direct violence and plantation discipline.
- Ashgrove owner: ownership, land, debt, and family separation.
- New Orleans trader: transport, auction, credit, and port commerce.
- County judge Elias Vane: legal protection and property claims.
- Regional militia captain: patrols, reprisals, and organized terror.

The list is not an assassination checklist. Every target has personal, structural, evidence, and consequence dimensions. A target can be killed, exposed, captured, leveraged, or otherwise neutralized depending on the mission design.

## Elias Vane twist

Elias Vane appears to have enabled Isaiah Coffin's sale. Later evidence shows that Vane altered or delayed documents to prevent one transfer and protect Isaiah from a worse route. Vane remains complicit: he validated other sales, protected owners, and benefited from the legal system. His actions create a moral conflict rather than a redemption arc.

The player may need Vane's records or testimony. Whether Ezra kills, exposes, uses, or condemns him depends on accumulated evidence, relationships, and community judgement.

## Historical divergence

The game uses real historical context and a fictional regional divergence after 1831. A fictional private convention of landowners, merchants, judges, and patrol representatives coordinates records, transport, debt, and pursuit to stabilize the regional slave system. This organization is fictional and must not be presented as a real historical body.

The divergence changes the fate of the fictional regional network, not the national history of slavery, the Civil War, or emancipation.

## Design pillars

- Human agency: enslaved and free Black characters have goals, expertise, relationships, and disagreements.
- Systemic clarity: slavery appears as law, finance, land, labor, transport, policing, and violence.
- Revenge with consequences: violence can be emotionally meaningful but never solves the system by itself.
- Collective resistance: the final achievement belongs to a network, not a lone savior.
- Deterministic outcomes: named-character survival, main objectives, dates, routes, and final campaign outcome are fixed.
- Historical responsibility: fact, reconstruction, fiction, and open questions are labelled separately.

## Content and rating

Target rating: Mature / PEGI 18. Content includes slavery, coercion, family separation, racial violence, captivity, pursuit, and historical trauma. Accessibility controls should cover violence, pursuit dogs, captivity, family separation, and slurs.

## Disclaimer

This is historical fiction. It uses real places, institutions, economic structures, and historical events as context. The protagonist, principal antagonists, plantation, resistance network, and direct plot are fictional. The game does not claim that Ezra ended slavery in the United States.
