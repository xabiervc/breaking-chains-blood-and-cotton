# Factions and Conflicts

## BC_FACTION_ASHGROVE_NETWORK

A fictional regional coordination network linking plantation owners, merchants, legal officials, transport operators, and patrol representatives. It exists to stabilize labor control and suppress escape.

## BC_FACTION_ENSLAVED_COMMUNITIES

Distinct communities with different goals, skills, risk tolerances, and internal disagreements. They are not a single faction or passive population.

## BC_FACTION_FREE_BLACK_COMMUNITIES

Churches, boatmen, artisans, families, and organizers who provide information, shelter, transport, medicine, and political judgement.

## BC_FACTION_ESCAPE_NETWORK

An informal, decentralized network of routes, refuges, conductors, healers, printers, and community decisions. It is not a literal railway or a single organization.

## BC_FACTION_PLANTATION_AUTHORITIES

Owners, overseers, managers, and hired specialists who control land, labor, food, tools, and records.

## BC_FACTION_PATROLS

Fictional coordination of slave patrols, militias, bounty hunters, informants, and local officials. The faction is period-appropriate and is not the post-1865 Ku Klux Klan.

## BC_FACTION_MERCHANTS

River, port, warehouse, credit, insurance, and transport interests that convert labor and people into financial value.

## BC_FACTION_ABOLITIONIST_NETWORK

Printers, correspondents, religious communities, organizers, and allies with different politics and risks. The network supports resistance but does not own its leadership.

## Core conflicts

- Escape versus surveillance.
- Personal revenge versus collective safety.
- Evidence versus immediate action.
- Compromised help versus moral purity.
- Local victory versus national continuity.
- Community trust versus operational secrecy.
