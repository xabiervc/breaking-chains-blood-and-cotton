# Character Bible

## BC_CHAR_EZRA_COFFIN

Ezra is a fictional 28-year-old enslaved blacksmith, toolmaker, wagon repairer, and occasional farrier from Ashgrove. He is observant, controlled, practical, technically skilled, emotionally guarded, and increasingly willing to risk himself for others.

Arc: escape, revenge, investigation, judgement, organization.

## BC_CHAR_ISAIAH_COFFIN

Ezra's younger brother was sold before the game begins. His status remains uncertain for much of the campaign. Isaiah is not merely a rescue objective; he has his own survival strategies, relationships, and views about Ezra's methods.

## BC_CHAR_MARA_BELL

A fictional conductor and community organizer. Mara teaches Ezra that escape depends on trust, logistics, care, and collective decisions rather than individual violence.

## BC_CHAR_RUTH_BELL

A fictional healer and safe-house coordinator in the Great Dismal Swamp region. Ruth has authority grounded in medical knowledge, local relationships, and risk management.

## BC_CHAR_THOMAS_VALE

A fictional free Black boatman controlling a dangerous river route. He distrusts armed resistance until Ezra demonstrates that the network can protect civilians.

## BC_CHAR_ELIAS_MERCER

A fictional white Quaker farmer and abolitionist contact. He offers one route and printing resources but never becomes the movement's moral centre.

## BC_CHAR_JOSEPHINE_REED

A fictional newspaper editor in the northern corridor. She turns testimony and evidence into public pressure while retaining editorial judgement and political risk.

## BC_CHAR_ELIAS_VANE

A fictional county judge. He represents legal transformation of people into property and institutional protection. He delayed or altered documents to protect Isaiah from one transfer, while continuing to validate and benefit from slavery. He is compromised, not redeemed.

## Other targets

The Ashgrove owner represents ownership, land, debt, violence, and local political power. The New Orleans trader represents auctions, shipping, credit, insurance, and interstate trafficking. The regional militia captain represents patrols, reprisals, and suppression after 1831. All principal antagonists are fictional composites.
