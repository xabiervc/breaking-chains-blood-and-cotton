# Story Bible

## Logline

After escaping Ashgrove Plantation in the aftermath of the 1831 Southampton uprising, Ezra Coffin hunts the people he believes destroyed his family. His revenge list gradually becomes an investigation into a regional system of ownership, trafficking, law, debt, and patrol violence, forcing him to choose between personal justice and collective survival.

## Narrative promise

The player receives the emotional momentum of a revenge thriller, the scale of an open-world journey, and the growing realization that no individual target explains the whole system.

## Point of divergence

The fictional Ashgrove network coordinates regional records, transport, finance, patrols, and legal protection after 1831. The network is not a historical organization. It is a fictional mechanism that allows the game to explore how existing institutions could cooperate around a regional crisis.

## Revenge arc

Ezra begins with five names. Each target initially appears to be a personal enemy. Investigation reveals the target's structural role, the evidence they control, and the regional consequences of removing them.

The arc changes from revenge to truth, then from truth to judgement, and finally from judgement to organized resistance.

## Elias Vane twist

Vane altered or delayed documents that protected Isaiah from one transfer. He still validated other sales and profited from the system. The reveal must not absolve him or make him the hidden hero. It must force Ezra and the affected communities to decide how to use a compromised source.

## Ending

The regional network connected to Ashgrove is dismantled. 340 people are liberated in the full campaign. The epilogue acknowledges that slavery continues nationally and that the fictional victory contributes to, but does not cause, the later historical movement toward abolition.
