# Breaking Chains: Blood and Cotton

A historically grounded, mature open-world action-adventure about escape, revenge, resistance, and the regional machinery of slavery in the antebellum United States.

## Status

Early pre-production. This repository currently contains the narrative, historical, design, technical, and validation foundations of the project.

## Canonical setting

- United States, 1830–1834.
- Opening context: the aftermath of Nat Turner's Rebellion in Southampton County, Virginia, August 1831.
- Protagonist: Ezra Coffin, a fictional enslaved blacksmith, age 28.
- Four technical acts: Escape, The Hunt, Reckoning, and Breaking the System.
- Five connected regions covering approximately 45 square miles.
- Canonical campaign outcome: a regional victory and 340 people liberated, not national abolition.

## Narrative identity

The campaign begins as a revenge story inspired by a target-list structure. Ezra pursues the people he believes destroyed his family. The list gradually becomes an investigation into the legal, economic, logistical, and violent system that made those crimes possible.

One target, the fictional county judge Elias Vane, appears responsible for Isaiah Coffin's sale. Later evidence shows that Vane altered or delayed documents to protect Isaiah from one transfer. Vane is not innocent: he continued to validate sales and benefit from the system. His ambiguity is central to the game's moral and structural design.

The narrative uses fictional regional divergences inside a real historical context. Historical fact, scholarly reconstruction, fictional invention, and open questions must always be distinguished.

## Repository principles

- All repository content is written in English.
- Historical claims require sources.
- Ezra is never presented as Nat Turner, Harriet Tubman, Frederick Douglass, or another historical person.
- Enslaved and free Black characters have independent agency.
- White allies do not become the center of the liberation story.
- Gameplay-critical outcomes are deterministic.
- Rescued people are represented as people with destinations, needs, relationships, and protection requirements, not collectibles.

## Disclaimer

This is a work of historical fiction. It uses real places, institutions, economic structures, and historical events as context. The protagonist, principal antagonists, plantation, resistance network, and direct plot are fictional. The game does not claim that its protagonist ended slavery in the United States.
